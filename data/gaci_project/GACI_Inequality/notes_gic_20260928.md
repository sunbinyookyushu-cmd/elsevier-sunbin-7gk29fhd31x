## 1. Stacked system with formal tests (`12b_gic_stacked.do` → `_gic_stacked_results.csv`)
Country × group × year panel (12 WID groups: d1–d10, top 1%, top 0.1%; N = 44,564; 163 clusters).
ln(avg pretax income of group) on group × ln GACI_max and group × ln pop, country#group FE, year#group FE, country-clustered SE.

First stage: per-equation KP F 10.5 (Feyrer). Stacked 12-endogenous system: Cragg–Donald F 135; the cluster-robust KP rk statistic is degenerate (returns 0.000) with 12 instruments × 163 clusters, so per-equation F is the relevant strength measure. Trend spec (2 endogenous): KP F 5.2.

Reading: 2SLS point estimates rise monotonically from d4 (0.54) to d10 (1.44) and top 1% (1.29), but the decile elasticities are not statistically distinguishable from each other; the only contrast at conventional levels is top half vs bottom half (p=.09). OLS gives a significant d10−d1 gap (p=.036) and top1%−d10 (p=.074). Continent×year and tourism-IV versions are uninformative (F 1.2 and 1.7). Report as: "the incidence curve slopes upward in point estimates; the slope is imprecisely estimated under the Feyrer instrument and disappears with continent-by-year effects."

## 2. Descriptive growth-by-group curves (`14_fig_gic_descriptive.py` → `fig_gic_descriptive`, `_gic_descriptive.csv`)
One observation per country: annualised Δln of each group's average pretax income and of ln GACI_max, first→last complete year (span ≥ 15 y; 138 countries, median span 26 y). Terciles by GACI growth: low mean −0.17 %/yr, mid 0.58, high 1.44.

| group | low tercile %/yr | high tercile %/yr | high − low (se) |
|---|---|---|---|
| p0–10 | 1.60 | 2.80 | 1.20 (0.58) |
| p40–50 | 1.04 | 2.58 | 1.54 (0.38) |
| p90–100 | 0.65 | 2.76 | 2.11 (0.37) |
| top 1% | 0.52 | 2.95 | 2.44 (0.44) |
| top 0.1% | 0.43 | 3.31 | 2.88 (0.58) |

Low-GACI-growth countries: growth declines from bottom to top (pro-poor). High-GACI-growth countries: flat across deciles, rising in the top fractiles. The high−low gap rises monotonically from 1.2 to 2.9 pp/yr. Caveat: the level gap (~1.5 pp) is catch-up growth of the faster-globalising countries; only the slope is distributional. Use as the motivating figure before the regressions (Piketty–Saez–Zucman 2018 style), not as evidence on its own.

## Naming for the paper
Call it "incidence of hub connectivity across the income distribution" / "group-specific elasticities"; cite Egger–Nigai–Strecker (AER 2019) for the design, Lang–Tavares (JEI 2024) for the WID-decile version, Piketty–Saez–Zucman (QJE 2018) for the descriptive curve, Aghion et al. (REStud 2019) for the "top shares, not Gini" contrast. Do not call it a growth incidence curve in the text; footnote Ravallion–Chen (2003).
